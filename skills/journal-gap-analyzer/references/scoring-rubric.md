# Relative Journal Fit Scoring Rubric

The score is an **indicative relative benchmark score**.

It is not:
- an acceptance probability;
- a journal quality score;
- a guarantee of editorial fit;
- a substitute for qualitative judgement.

## Default weighted dimensions

| Dimension | Weight |
|---|---:|
| Scope Fit | 10 |
| Novelty Framing | 10 |
| Methodological Evidence | 15 |
| Results Depth | 15 |
| Discussion Depth | 15 |
| Literature Positioning | 10 |
| Claim Calibration | 10 |
| Reproducibility | 5 |
| Visual / Quantitative Communication | 5 |
| Journal Writing Architecture | 5 |

Total: 100

## Dimension scoring

Prefer 5-point increments.

### 90–100
Closely aligned with the central benchmark tendency and supported by strong evidence.

### 75–85
Broadly aligned; some local differences or modest gaps remain.

### 60–70
Partially aligned; one or more meaningful gaps are visible.

### 40–55
Material difference from benchmark tendency.

### 0–35
Large gap, incompatible evidence, or severe lack of comparable information.

If evidence is unavailable, use `Unable to assess` rather than assigning zero.

## Confidence modifier

Each dimension should also have:
- High confidence
- Medium confidence
- Low confidence

Confidence depends on both:
- manuscript evidence quality;
- benchmark evidence quality.

Do not mathematically inflate precision by converting Low-confidence qualitative judgements into decimal scores.

## Weight adjustments

Weights may change when justified.

Examples:

### Methods paper
Increase:
- Methodological Evidence
- Reproducibility

### Review article
Replace experimental/numerical evidence emphasis with:
- coverage
- synthesis
- critical comparison
- framework/taxonomy

### Short communication
Reduce penalties for breadth and manuscript length.

State material weight changes explicitly.

## Scoring principle

The score should follow the evidence-based comparison.

Do not choose a score first and then invent reasons to justify it.
