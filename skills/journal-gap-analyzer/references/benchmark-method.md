# Journal Benchmark Method

Build the benchmark before judging the manuscript.

## 1. Extract comparable features

For each benchmark paper, record only supported features.

Recommended fields:

- article type
- research scope
- contribution type
- abstract length and density
- introduction gap framing
- methods detail
- validation / uncertainty / statistics
- results quantitative density
- discussion depth
- literature comparison
- limitation handling
- claim strength
- reproducibility detail
- figure/table function
- conclusion compression

## 2. Separate formal rules from observed tendencies

Formal rule:
- comes from official journal guidance.

Observed tendency:
- appears in the sampled papers.

Never call a sampled tendency a journal requirement.

## 3. Use central tendency, not phrase imitation

Summarise patterns such as:
- most sampled papers use concise abstracts;
- Discussion sections typically include direct literature comparison;
- comparable numerical papers usually report quantitative validation errors.

Do not build a phrase bank.

Do not copy distinctive wording.

## 4. Track benchmark variability

For each dimension classify sample variability:
- Low
- Moderate
- High

High variability means the journal tolerates multiple approaches; widen the acceptable range and reduce the penalty for manuscript difference.

## 5. Evidence hierarchy

Strong benchmark evidence:
- recurring pattern across several comparable full-text papers.

Moderate benchmark evidence:
- pattern across a small sample or mixed methodologies.

Weak benchmark evidence:
- one paper, abstracts only, old sample, or low-comparability papers.

## 6. Do not reward superficial imitation

A manuscript should not score highly simply because:
- sentence lengths look similar;
- headings look similar;
- figure counts match;
- transition words resemble the target sample.

Scientific fit, evidence depth and discussion quality matter more than cosmetic similarity.

## 7. Benchmark summary

Before scoring, output:

- what the sampled papers consistently do;
- what varies substantially;
- what appears article-type specific;
- what cannot be inferred confidently.
